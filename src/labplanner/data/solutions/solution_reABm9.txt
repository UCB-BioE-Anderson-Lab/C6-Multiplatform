>>procedure
THIS IS reABm6 WITH ABm9's PART A. The lysate half is unchanged, step for step; the only
difference is that the part A it is mixed with carries 2% sucrose instead of 5%. Everything
reABm6 says about the lysis applies here.

1. Spin down the saturated culture and discard the spent supernatant. The BIOMASS is recycled,
   not the conditioned medium.
2. Resuspend the pellet in the water.
3. Add the NaOH. Hold warm until the suspension goes transparent -- transparency is the
   endpoint, not a time. NaOH was the only agent of about twelve screened that reached it.
4. Neutralize with the H3PO4, then add the NH4Cl and the KCl.
5. Bring part B to volume, then mix equal parts A and B. Add antibiotic.

WHY THIS AND NOT reABm6 OR reABm1 -- IT IS THE ONE WHOSE PART A IS SHARED. All three are the same
idea; what separates them is the sugar and where the acid goes.

  reABm6   ABm6 part A unchanged, 5% sucrose. Matches ABm6, so an ABm6 arm differs in one thing.
  reABm1   1% sucrose AND the acid premixed into part A, so lysis and neutralization are one step.
  reABm9   ABm9 part A unchanged, 2% sucrose. Matches ABm9, and it is the SAME BOTTLE -- the
           bench makes one part A and pours it into both media.

reABm1's premix is the thing given up here: with a plain part A the phosphate goes in on the
lysate side as it does in reABm6 and Lys100. ABm5.xlsx's reAB1 sheet anticipated that fallback --
*"It may become necessary to put in the phosphate separate from the others as done with Lys100"*.

MEASURE THE pH AND WRITE IT DOWN. It has never been measured in any run of a lysate medium --
"I didn't check any of the pH's", "probably more like pH 6 based on litmus". There is no pass band
because nobody has established one; the reading is the point.

SCALING IS BY CULTURE VOLUME, as for reABm6. The unit here is 50 mL of medium from 50 mL of
culture: the cells end up 2x concentrated, because 50 mL of culture makes only 25 mL of part B. So
the culture volume you need equals the medium volume you want.
