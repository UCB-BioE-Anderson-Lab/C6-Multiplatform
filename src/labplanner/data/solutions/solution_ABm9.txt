>solution	ABm9
>description	"ABm6 at 2% sucrose instead of 5%, so one part A serves both the M9 and the lysate medium. Mix equal parts A and B."
>volume	0.5
>source	JCA, 2026-11-02; ABm6 from Pimar ABm5.xlsx, sheet "ABm6"

>>composition	amt	units	note
part A	250	mL	make to half the final volume; this half is 2x
sucrose	10	g	4% w/v in part A, 2% w/v final
2M MgSO4	2.5	mL	10 mM final -- unchanged from ABm6
1M CaCl2	333	uL	0.67 mM final -- unchanged from ABm6
trace metals	500	uL	1 mL per litre of FINISHED medium; see the note below
part B	250	mL	make to half the final volume
M9 salts	6	g	12 g/L final, i.e. about 1x -- unchanged from ABm6

>>procedure
Mix equal parts of A and B.

ONLY THE SUCROSE DIFFERS FROM ABm6. Mg, Ca, trace metals and the M9 half are the same, so a
result against ABm6 differs in one thing.

WHY IT EXISTS: ONE PART A FOR TWO MEDIA. ABm6's part A is 5% sucrose and reABm1's is 1%, so a
bench running both an M9 medium and a lysate medium kept two part A bottles at different sugar.
At 2% the same bottle feeds both, and `reABm9` is this part A with the M9 half replaced by
lysate. JCA, 2026-11-02: *"I don't want to base this on so much sucrose loading."*

WHAT IT COSTS, SAID PLAINLY. The ABm6 record is eight years of grows/dies calls taken at 5%
sucrose, and sucrose is the osmotic variable in this medium. A strain scored in ABm9 is NOT
directly comparable to those calls: it may be answering the sugar rather than the salts. Record
the sucrose alongside the medium name on any result, and do not read an ABm9 result as an ABm6
one. ABm6 is kept as its own record for exactly that reason.

Part A and part B are made and sterilized SEPARATELY, then combined once cool -- autoclaving
sucrose with phosphate caramelizes it, and Ca2+ and Mg2+ precipitate with phosphate.

TRACE METALS: WHICH SOLUTION, AND WHICH DOSE. 500 uL here is a 1000x concentrate at the label
dose of 1 mL per litre of finished medium, which is what Teknova's is. The lab's own
`trace_metal_soln` was historically dosed at 333 uL per 250 mL of part A, i.e. 0.67 mL per litre
finished -- see solution_ABm6. The two are different formulations and neither dose is wrong for
the other, so the figure moves with the bottle. Either way it is filter sterilized, not
autoclaved, and goes into part A after cooling.

M9 salts means the dry blend, as in ABm6: per ABm5.xlsx's "M9 Salts" sheet a 10x solution is 70 g
Na2HPO4.7H2O + 30 g KH2PO4 + 5 g NaCl + 10 g NH4Cl per L, so 12 g/L is about 1x. A commercial M9
powder may differ; check its own 1x mass per litre.

SCALING. The unit above is 500 mL. For 2 L, make 1 L of each part: part A takes 40 g sucrose,
10 mL 2M MgSO4, 1.33 mL 1M CaCl2 and 2 mL trace metals; part B takes 24 g M9 salts.
