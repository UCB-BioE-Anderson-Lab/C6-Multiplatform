>solution	ABm6
>description	"Sucrose minimal medium for E. coli. Mix equal parts A and B. 5% w/v sucrose final."
>volume	0.5
>source	Pimar ABm5.xlsx, sheet "ABm6"

>>composition	amt	units	note
part A	250	mL	make to half the final volume; this half is 2x
sucrose	25	g	5% w/v final
2M MgSO4	2.5	mL	10 mM final
1M CaCl2	333	uL	0.67 mM final
trace_metal_soln	333	uL	the "trace salts" line; see solution_trace-metal-soln
part B	250	mL	make to half the final volume
M9 salts	6	g	12 g/L final, i.e. about 1x

>>procedure
Mix equal parts of A and B.

Part A and part B are made and sterilized SEPARATELY, then combined once cool. The workbook
states the split without saying why; autoclaving sucrose together with phosphate caramelizes it,
and Ca2+ and Mg2+ precipitate with phosphate. Treat the split as the intent.

M9 salts here means the dry blend: per the workbook's "M9 Salts" sheet, a 10x solution is 70 g
Na2HPO4.7H2O + 30 g KH2PO4 + 5 g NaCl + 10 g NH4Cl per L, so 12 g/L is about 1x. If using a
commercial M9 powder, check its own 1x mass per litre -- the workbook's Sheet1 quotes 112.8 g/L
for 10x where the M9 Salts sheet gives 115, and the two do not quite agree.

trace_metal_soln is filter sterilized, not autoclaved, so it is added to part A after cooling.

SCALING. The unit above is 500 mL. For 2 L, make 1 L of each part: part A takes 100 g sucrose,
10 mL 2M MgSO4, 1.33 mL 1M CaCl2 and 1.33 mL trace_metal_soln; part B takes 24 g M9 salts.
