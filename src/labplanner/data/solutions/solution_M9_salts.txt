>>composition	amt	units	note
Na2HPO4.7H2O	70	g	CHECK THE HYDRATION ON YOUR BOTTLE -- see the procedure
KH2PO4	30	g	the only potassium in ABm6; do NOT substitute a sodium phosphate for it
NaCl	5	g
NH4Cl	10	g	the nitrogen source

>>procedure
This is the 10x blend: 115 g dissolved in 1 L is 10x M9, so 11.5 g/L is 1x. ABm6 calls for
12 g/L of the dry mix, which is 1x within the precision anybody weighs it to.

AS A DRY BLEND OR WEIGHED DIRECTLY, either works. For a batch of ABm6, weighing the four
components straight into part B saves making the blend at all -- for 24 g of mix, that is
14.6 g Na2HPO4.7H2O, 6.26 g KH2PO4, 1.04 g NaCl and 2.09 g NH4Cl.

HYDRATION IS THE USUAL WAY THIS GOES WRONG. 70 g is the HEPTAHYDRATE, MW 268.07. If the bottle
on the shelf is anhydrous Na2HPO4, MW 141.96, the same moles are 37.1 g per 10x litre -- a bit
over half the mass. The dodecahydrate, MW 358.14, is 93.5 g. Weighing out 70 g of anhydrous
gives nearly twice the phosphate the recipe intends, and nothing downstream will tell you.

THE POTASSIUM COMES FROM KH2PO4 AND NOWHERE ELSE in ABm6. A sodium phosphate is not a
substitute for it: the phosphate would be right and the medium would have no potassium in it.
(reABm6 is different -- it adds KCl to the lysate half, and reABm1 lyses with KOH for the same
reason.)

The workbook disagrees with itself by about 2% on what 10x means -- the M9 Salts sheet gives
115 g/L and Sheet1 quotes 112.8 g/L. Below the precision this matters at, recorded so nobody
re-derives it and wonders.
